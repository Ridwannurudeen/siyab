# The incident record

This document is policy and process. It says how an incident is recorded, what a page shows and what
it refuses to say. The data model itself is in `docs/RECORD.md`; this is the practice around it.

## What becomes a record

A record is opened when one of these holds: an exploit takes funds on NEAR or through NEAR Intents, a
key or a signer is compromised, an account is used to receive stolen funds, or a payment to a named
account was refused because of a report. A single unverified claim is not an incident: it is a
`pending` report, and pending only asks.

Each record states a `kind`, an `outcome` and its evidence. The outcome is not a guess about severity:
`loss` means funds or control were lost, `attempt` means someone tried and nothing was lost, and
`near_miss` means it was stopped or noticed in time. `unknown` is for a report that does not say, and
it never raises the outcome of a record on its own.

## Subjects are not victims

`subjects` holds the accounts agents should not pay. It never holds the victim. A victim's account is
part of what happened, and it belongs in the evidence and in the prose, not in the refusal set. If a
victim's account were listed, the product would tell agents to refuse the person who was robbed, and
the first real support ticket would be from them.

When we cannot tell which account was the collector, we do not publish a subject. We publish the
attempt, the evidence and the refusal, and we say that attribution is unverified. The 29 October
movement through NEAR is recorded that way: an attempt with a loss elsewhere, no named NEAR subject.

## Timestamps

Every timestamp is a date plus the source it came from, and none is inferred.

- `first_chain_signal` is a block height we read ourselves, and the height is in the entry.
- `first_public_report` is the earliest public writing we found, with its url. If we found none, the
  entry is absent. We never write "first reported" without a link.
- `our_record` is when we opened the record, and it is not a claim about when the event happened.
- `moderation` and `status_change` carry the decision id.

A timeline that reads backwards is a bug, not a rounding error; the validator refuses it.

## Corrections

Nothing is edited. A correction is a new record that supersedes the earlier one, with a `superseded`
entry explaining what changed. The earlier record keeps its bytes, its hash and its state, and the
store marks it superseded so a reader knows it is history rather than current.

We correct three things this way: a wrong account id, a wrong outcome, and a wrong kind. A wrong
subject is the most serious, because a refusal is the only thing here that costs someone money, and
it is corrected within one business day of us confirming it.

## What a page shows

Each incident page carries the kind, the outcome, the state, the subjects with their state beside
them, the timeline with sources, the evidence documents with their hashes, and the decisions with
their readable reasons. A page never shows a count we have not computed from committed files, and
every number on it is regenerated by a script rather than typed.

A record in `accepted` shows as blocked for the five denial kinds, and a `social` record in
`accepted` shows as asking. A `contested` record keeps the answer it had when it was accepted:
blocked for the five denial kinds, asking for `social`, with the reason beside it, because a
dispute must not switch a label off. A record appealed from `rejected` held no answer to keep, so
it shows as asking while we read the appeal. A record in `pending` shows as asking. A record in
`withdrawn`, `rejected` or `overturned` shows as no effect: none of them is a label any more, and
each stays visible with its history and the decision that closed it.

## Publication

We publish an incident when the record is accepted, or when we hold an attempt that a reader needs in
order to judge a payment. We do not publish an unverified attribution as fact, we do not name a person,
and we do not publish a victim's account as a subject.

We publish our own misses. If an incident is reported publicly before we recorded it, the record says
so in its timeline, and the monthly measurement counts the gap. A record that only appears once we
already knew the answer is not a detection, and we do not present it as one.

## The monthly measured page

Once a month we publish the times from `intel/service_times.json` as measured, per record: how long
from the earliest public report to our record, from our record to a decision, and from an appeal to
its answer. The misses stay in. A monthly page that only shows the good months is marketing, and
nobody signs a contract on marketing.

## What we will not say

We will not say an account is criminal, only that it carries a record with a source. We will not say
a person controls an account. We will not say a payment is illegal. And we will not describe a
refusal as proof that the other side was at fault: a refusal means our rules found a signal, and the
signal has a citation, which is exactly what a reader needs to disagree with us.
